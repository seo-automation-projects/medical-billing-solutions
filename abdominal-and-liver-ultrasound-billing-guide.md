# Diagnostic Abdominal & Hepatobiliary Ultrasound Billing: Complete vs. Limited Study Documentation (CPT 76700 & 76705)

Diagnostic ultrasound claims for hepatobiliary and abdominal imaging face frequent payer scrutiny, downcoding, and medical necessity denials when radiology reports fail to meet complete organ-set documentation criteria. Accurate reimbursement depends on distinguishing between a complete abdominal ultrasound and a focused, single-organ or quadrant-limited study, alongside proper professional (-26) and technical (-TC) component modifiers.

## 1. Complete Abdominal Ultrasound (CPT 76700) vs. Limited Study (CPT 76705)

Under American Medical Association (AMA) CPT and American College of Radiology (ACR) coding guidelines, choosing between CPT 76700 and CPT 76705 hinges on the anatomical scope required by the clinical order and documented in the final radiology interpretation:

- **Complete Abdominal Ultrasound (CPT 76700):** Requires permanently recorded images and written evaluation of all eight anatomical structures: liver, gallbladder, common bile duct, pancreas, spleen, kidneys, and the upper abdominal aorta and inferior vena cava.
- **Limited Abdominal Ultrasound (CPT 76705):** Reported when the exam evaluates a single organ (such as a standalone hepatobiliary/liver evaluation), a specific abdominal quadrant (such as right upper quadrant pain), or a targeted follow-up check.
- **Abdominal/Visceral Duplex Scan (CPT 93975 / 93976):** Reported separately only when true spectral and color Doppler velocity waveform analysis is medically necessary and performed to assess portal vein patency or hepatic vascular flow.

If even one required structure of a complete abdominal study cannot be visualized (for example, due to bowel gas or a prior cholecystectomy), the radiology report must explicitly document the clinical reason for non-visualization; otherwise, payers will downcode CPT 76700 to CPT 76705 during post-payment review.

## 2. Applying Professional (-26), Technical (-TC), and Repeat Study Modifiers

Outpatient imaging centers, hospital radiology departments, and office-based gastroenterology or hepatology practices must append accurate billing modifiers based on ownership of the ultrasound equipment and the interpreting physician's setting:

- **Global Service Billing (No Component Modifier):** Used only when the same practice or entity owns the ultrasound equipment, employs the sonographer, and provides the physician's written interpretation and report in an office setting (Place of Service 11).
- **Professional Component (Modifier 26):** Appended when the physician provides only the clinical supervision and final written interpretation for an exam performed on hospital or facility-owned equipment (such as POS 21, 22, or 23).
- **Technical Component (Modifier TC):** Appended by the facility or independent diagnostic testing facility (IDTF) to capture equipment, supplies, and sonographer staffing costs.
- **Distinct Procedural Service (Modifier 59 / XU):** Rarely applicable between CPT 76700 and CPT 76705 on the same date of service, as National Correct Coding Initiative (NCCI) edits bundle a limited abdominal ultrasound into a complete abdominal study unless performed at a separate session for a distinct clinical change.

## 3. Advanced Liver Elastography (CPT 91200) and Steatosis Screening

When evaluating patients with chronic liver disease, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD/NAFLD), cirrhosis, or viral hepatitis, practices frequently perform transient elastography or shear-wave liver elastography (CPT 91200) to measure hepatic stiffness non-invasively.

To prevent bundling rejections when billing liver elastography alongside diagnostic B-mode imaging:
- **Verify Payer LCDs for Same-Day Imaging:** Check whether the local Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC) or commercial payer allows CPT 91200 to be billed on the same date as CPT 76705, or whether separate clinical indications are required.
- **Pair High-Specificity ICD-10-CM Codes:** Avoid unspecified abdominal pain codes (R10.9) when specific clinical findings exist; link elevated liver transaminases (R74.01), fatty liver (K76.0), chronic viral hepatitis C (B18.2), or hepatic fibrosis (K74.00–K74.02) directly to the imaging line item.
- **Maintain Permanent Image Archives:** Both CMS and commercial payers require permanently recorded B-mode and elastography images alongside a signed interpretive report in the medical record.
